Toast

Toasts confirm what just happened, at the edge of the screen, and go away on their own. Errors stay until dismissed.

WCAG 2.2 AAStatus: stableLive region: status / alert

Overview

A toast confirms that something the person just did has happened, without taking them away from what they were doing: an event added to Google Calendar, filters cleared, a submission approved. It slides in at the edge of the screen, waits, then goes away by itself.

  • Use a toast for the result of an action the person took on this screen.
  • Use an alert for a problem or fact tied to a place on the page (a sold-out event, a form error), and a site banner for something that affects the whole site (planned maintenance, lost connection).
  • Use a dialog when the person must decide before continuing.
  • Never use a toast for the only copy of important information; it disappears. Moderation results also appear in the notification list.

Anatomy

  1. Tone icon20px, coloured with --color-{tone}-icon-on-inverse, aria-hidden; the title carries the meaning.
  2. Title--text-label; what happened, in the past tense.
  3. Text (optional)--text-body-sm; the object or the next step.
  4. Action (optional)One text button: Undo, Compare, Try again.
  5. Dismiss24px icon button labelled "Dismiss".
  6. Timer bar2px, currentColor at 40%; shows the time left; hidden on errors, which never time out.

Variants

The tone matches the outcome. Every tone uses the same inverse surface so toasts stand apart from the page; only the icon colour changes.

  • Auto-dismiss: info and success after 8 seconds, warning after 12. The timer pauses while the pointer is over the toast or focus is inside it, and while the window is hidden.
  • Danger never auto-dismisses and has no timer bar.
  • Leaving fades out over --duration-base; the next toast moves up.

Responsive behaviour

  • Below --layout-breakpoint-sm (640px) the region spans the viewport, inset by --space-4 on both sides, at the bottom.
  • From 640px the region is 24rem wide, anchored bottom-right with --space-6 from the edges.
  • .toast-region--top moves it to the top on screens where a bottom bar (the sticky "Get tickets" CTA on event detail) would cover it.
  • Fixed bottom placement adds env(safe-area-inset-bottom) on phones with a home indicator, and the region never covers the focused element (WCAG 2.4.11).

Theming

Toasts sit on --color-bg-inverse with --color-fg-inverse text: ink on the light theme, near-white on the dark theme. That keeps them distinct from both the canvas and raised surfaces. The tone colours only the icon, through --color-info-icon-on-inverse, --color-success-icon-on-inverse, --color-warning-icon-on-inverse and --color-danger-icon-on-inverse. These take the opposite theme's icon colour, because the regular dark-theme icons (the -300 steps) measured 1.5–1.9:1 on the light inverse surface. The focus ring inside a toast uses --color-focus-ring-on-inverse for the same reason.

/* A product that wants tinted toasts overrides the surface per tone, not the component CSS. */
.toast--danger { background: var(--color-danger-bg); color: var(--color-danger-fg); }

Elevation is --shadow-3; the dark theme's stronger shadow keeps the toast lifted off dark surfaces.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

A persistent live region exists in the page from load: <div class="toast-region" role="status" aria-live="polite">. Toasts are inserted into it, so screen readers announce the title and text without moving focus (WCAG 4.1.3). Errors go into a second region with role="alert" (assertive). Never put a role="alert" toast inside the polite region; nested live regions are announced twice or not at all.

Keyboard

KeyAction
Tab / Shift+TabToasts are not in the focus order until the person reaches them; the action and dismiss buttons are normal buttons in DOM order (the region sits at the end of <body>).
Enter, SpaceActivates the action or dismisses.
EscWith focus inside a toast, dismisses it and returns focus to the element that triggered it.
F6Optional landmark jump to the toast region (labelled “Notifications”).

Focus

Showing a toast never moves focus. When a focused toast is dismissed, focus returns to the control that caused it (the "Add to calendar" button), or to <main> if that control is gone. The dismiss button shows the shared focus ring, drawn in --color-focus-ring-on-inverse, which is checked against the inverse surface below.

Labelling

The title and text are the announcement; keep them short. The dismiss button is labelled "Dismiss". Action buttons name the action ("Undo", "Try again"); when several toasts can show, add the object visually hidden ("Undo clearing filters").

Motion

Toasts slide up 16px and fade in over --duration-base with --ease-enter, and fade out over --duration-base with --ease-exit. Under prefers-reduced-motion the tokens zero the durations, so toasts appear and disappear without movement. The timer still runs.

Timing

At least 8 seconds visible (WCAG 2.2.1), paused on hover and focus, and errors never time out.

Touch

The dismiss button is 24px (--target-min) with at least 8px from the action. On phones the whole toast can also be swiped down to dismiss; the dismiss button stays as the single-pointer alternative (WCAG 2.5.7).

Content

  • Title: what happened, past tense, sentence case, no full stop: "Added to Google Calendar", "Filters cleared", "Approved with a possible duplicate".
  • Text: the object or the consequence: "Azure Data & AI Day GTA, Thu Nov 5.", "Showing all 14 events."
  • Error titles start with "Couldn’t" and name the thing: "Couldn’t copy the link". The text says what to do next.
  • Event titles go in curly quotes: “Rust in production at scale”.
  • Never "Success!", "Oops" or "Error". Never a toast for something the person did not do.
